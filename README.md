- No tiny text; good spacing between sections
- Code-style output format for allocator decision

**Speaker Notes:**
"The architecture has one cardinal rule: AI explains, deterministic engines decide, humans approve. Financial arithmetic never goes through the LLM. The allocator is pure greedy logic—reserve buffer, pay Tier-1 in full or fail, allocate remaining to Tier-2 in priority order. The LLM tells the owner why each obligation is in its tier and what the consequences are. But the decision—how much to pay whom—is rule-based and auditable. The event monitor is a single listener, not multiple agents. When a customer says their receivable is 3 days late, one monitor detects it, marks the original plan invalid, and triggers a full replan. Maximum one refinement retry if consequences are high-risk; otherwise, escalate to human. That's how we avoid over-optimizing and keep the human in the loop."

The system wakes up. It detects this as material—₹30K missing from the pipeline. It re-reasons: what's the new liquidity state? It re-allocates: we can't pay Logistics fully now; we'll protect salary and GST, reduce Raw Material payment to 17%, and tell that supplier why. It simulates: high ops risk on Raw Material, but low on Logistics. It asks the owner: approve this new plan or override? Owner says yes, and adds a note: 'Already called the supplier.' Payments execute. The system keeps watching. Friday, the receivable lands—now we replan again to pay back the deferred obligations. This is agentic: the system autonomously detects change, reasons, plans, simulates, escalates to human, executes, and waits for the next trigger. No human has to wake up at 10:15 AM and recalculate."
